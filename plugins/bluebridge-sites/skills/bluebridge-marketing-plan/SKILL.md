# Plan my marketing

Read [access and capability rules](references/access.md). Call `get_capabilities`, select a returned organization, then use the real `get_marketing_context` tool. This workflow does not require selecting a website. Load the current plan and relevant supporting documents with `list_marketing_documents`, `search_marketing_documents`, `read_marketing_documents` or `read_marketing_range`. The old `get_organization_context` name remains a mock. Use `get_marketing_history` when earlier decisions or unfinished saves matter.

On resuming, load the decision record as well as the plan. Use client-facing language from the access rules: omit storage architecture/file inventories and technical governance from the marketing summary. Start with a short account of what is known, relevant pending decisions (especially those blocking the requested work), and the important gaps. Do not reopen settled decisions without new evidence or a user request. Ask focused questions as the conversation develops; do not repeat questions answered in the saved plan or present a long generic questionnaire. Explore business outcomes, audience needs, positioning, offers/services, discovery/referral channels, budget, available expertise and production capacity. Ask about the client’s expertise/review availability and business constraints; separate that contribution from Blue Bridge’s platform and measurement work. Research with browsing or connected tools when available and useful; Blue Bridge is responsible for measurement setup/integration and reporting; this does not establish that measurement data is connected or available to this conversation. Use actual capability/data evidence and treat unverified setup/baselines as Blue Bridge follow-up work, rather than asking the client who will run analytics. Record source/date and distinguish evidence from interpretation.

Develop priorities together, explaining reasons, tradeoffs and what evidence would change the recommendation. Keep client-confirmed decisions, working proposals and unresolved questions separate. Do not turn a suggestion into a confirmed decision or invent results, credentials, testimonials, forecasts or performance measurements. Strategy agreement does not approve website/legal claims for publication.

Treat decisions as shared memory, not a prerequisite for every change. Make authorized routine edits within the agreed direction—wording, organization, research and task breakdown—without creating a decision record or asking for repeated approval. Record meaningful choices about audiences, positioning, priorities, budget, channels or significant commitments. A request to draft or explore something authorizes that work; it does not by itself confirm the strategic recommendation.

Maintain the existing decision register, normally `strategy/decisions.md`. Give each decision a stable ID (for example `DEC-001`), title, status (`pending`, `decided`, `deferred`, `rejected` or `superseded`), question/options, recommendation with rationale/evidence, decision owner, and created/updated dates. For resolved choices record the outcome, who actually decided and when, and the supporting conversation confirmation or document reference. Record a review trigger/date and related work or decision IDs when useful. Mark missing attribution/dates as unknown; never infer the decision maker from the logged-in saver, commit author or document owner, or use the save date as an unknown decision date. Use ISO dates; include time and timezone only when known and useful. Git/save history records who persisted the document, separately from who made the decision.

List pending choices first in the register. Link dependent work to decision IDs, but keep independent work moving. When requested work depends on an unresolved choice, ask for the choice or, when authorized, proceed with a clearly recorded reversible assumption and its impact; do not silently mark it decided. Preserve prior outcomes when direction changes: mark the old record superseded, create/link the replacement, and retain who decided what and when. Record rejected/deferred outcomes with their attribution and rationale; leaving a choice open is not agreement. Keep ordinary research questions in the questions document rather than manufacturing decisions for every unknown.

Preserve useful developing work when the user intends to create/update the plan; respect requests to brainstorm without saving. Use the document paths/conventions returned by context, reusing existing documents rather than creating duplicate plans. Markdown may include small YAML frontmatter fields such as status, owner and update date. Save the plan, decisions, open questions and next steps needed for another session, rather than a transcript of the conversation. Only label a decision agreed when the user has actually agreed. Concrete topics/briefs belong with bluebridge-content-plan.

When `save_marketing_changeset` is real and `permissions.save` is true, save related document changes together with the current `expectedCommit`, a unique idempotency key and expected full-document SHA-256 for every replacement; use null only for new files. A range returns `fileSha256` for the full document and `sha256` for the slice: never use the slice hash as the expected document hash. Read the complete document before replacing it, following all ranges where needed, and preserve unrelated material. Changes are exact full-text replacements/creates within the registered planning directory, not arbitrary repository writes.

A successful response with `status: saved` establishes the saved commit. Explain what was saved and which questions remain. After an unknown outcome, query `get_marketing_operation` if its ID is known (the original actor can use `includeChangeset` to recover exact `retryInput` in a new session), or repeat the exact original changeset and key to reconcile; do not generate a new key or content for a retry. On conflict, reload context/files, reconcile intentionally and use a new key. Pending/prepared is not saved. On a fresh session, reread authoritative context and continue from the saved plan/questions.

If saving is unavailable, explain the missing capability or role and provide a clearly labeled conversation-only draft. Do not collect credentials or imply it will survive into another session. A planning save creates no website build, candidate, approval, social schedule or publication.
